Few questions reach my inbox as often as this one. The honest answer: the certificate matters less than the **sequence** and less again than the **non-certificate skills**. But certs open doors and set a shared vocabulary, so let's get them straight.

## The three, compared

| | **CRISC** | **CISM** | **CISSP** |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Body | ISACA | ISACA | ISC2 |
| Focus | **IT risk** & control | **Security management** | Broad **security** (technical + managerial) |
| Angle | Risk, governance, controls | Program, governance, incident mgmt | Architecture, ops, and risk |
| Domains | 4 | 4 | 8 |
| Experience required | ~3 years | ~5 years (management-weighted) | ~5 years |
| CPE | ~20/year (ISACA) | ~20/year (ISACA) | ~40/year (ISC2) |
| Best for | Risk analysts, GRC, auditors, IT risk managers | Aspiring security managers/CISOs | Security engineers moving up or across |

*(Experience requirements and CPE are approximate and do change — confirm on ISACA's and ISC2's sites before you commit.)*

### CRISC — the risk specialist's flag

Domains: **Governance · IT Risk Assessment · Risk Response and Reporting · Information Technology and Security.**

This is *the* certification for people whose job is IT risk — assessment, treatment, reporting, and control. If you are building a risk register, running assessments, or sitting in a GRC function, CRISC is the most on-target of the three. It also pairs naturally with the material on this blog.

### CISM — for the would-be manager

Domains: **Security Governance · Information Risk Management · Security Program Development and Management · Security Incident Management.**

CISM assumes you are heading toward **managing** security: governing a programme, owning risk to the business, running incidents. It is management-weighted, which is why the experience requirement is steeper and more senior.

### CISSP — the broad, deep one

Eight domains spanning **security and risk management, asset security, architecture, network security, IAM, assessment, operations, and software security.**

CISSP is the widest and the most demanding to study for. It is excellent if you want breadth, respect across the industry, and a springboard from engineering into architecture, consulting, or management. It is less *specific* to risk practice than CRISC.

## Which one, and when

| Your situation | Start with |
| --- | --- |
| Risk/GRC/audit role, want to specialise in IT risk | **CRISC** |
| Security background, heading toward management/CISO | **CISM** (CISSP also fine) |
| Security engineer, want breadth and credibility | **CISSP** |
| Early career, no certs yet | Whichever matches your **current role** — certs validate a path, they don't create one |
| Wants all three | CRISC first (closest to risk work), then CISM, then CISSP if breadth is owed |

A pragmatic sequence many risk professionals follow: **CRISC early** (it matches the work), **CISM later** (when you are managing), and **CISSP** if they need industry-wide breadth.

## The ladder, honestly

Certifications help at the **entry and lateral** rungs. Beyond that, promotions turn on other things:

```
Analyst  →  Risk Specialist  →  Risk Manager  →  Head of Risk / CISO
  ▲              ▲                  ▲                    ▲
 certs help ─────┘          ownership helps ─────┘   influence helps ──┘
```

- **Analyst → Specialist:** a certification plus demonstrable hands-on work (a register you run, assessments you lead).
- **Specialist → Manager:** you now own a programme, a team, or a budget. Impact matters more than another acronym.
- **Manager → Head/CISO:** communication, judgement, and relationships with the executive and board dominate.

## What the certs don't teach — and what actually gets you promoted

- **Communication.** Explaining risk to a non-technical executive in one page beats being right and unheard.
- **Business acumen.** Understanding strategy, revenue, and cost so risk is a trade-off, not a prohibition.
- **Frameworks fluency.** Knowing when to apply [ISO, NIST, COBIT](/posts/it-risk-frameworks-explained/) — and when to be pragmatic.
- **Data and quantification.** [FAIR-style thinking](/posts/fair-in-15-minutes/) and honest metrics make you credible with finance.
- **Stakeholder management.** Risk is delivered by other people; influence is the job.
- **Judgement.** Knowing which risk is worth escalating and which to absorb — the skill no exam assesses.

## Practical advice

- **Match the cert to your current role**, not your dream role, if you want it to pay off quickly.
- **Pair studying with doing.** Apply each domain to a real register or assessment. Retention follows application.
- **Budget for CPE.** Certifications are maintained, not owned forever.
- **Don't collect logos.** Three letters you can't apply look worse than one you can.
- **Build the portfolio.** Write, present, automate a metric, improve a process. Visible work outperforms certificates at every rung above specialist.

## Key takeaways

- **CRISC = IT risk specialist's cert; CISM = security management; CISSP = broad security.**
- **Sequence by role:** CRISC early for risk work, CISM when managing, CISSP for breadth.
- Experience requirements and CPE vary — **verify with ISACA/ISC2** before planning.
- Certifications **open doors; skills and impact get you promoted**.
- Invest deliberately in **communication, business framing, quantification, and influenceship**.
